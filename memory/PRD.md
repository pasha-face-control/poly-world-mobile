# HexTribes — Polytopia-style 4X (Expo/React Native)

## Original Problem
Build a game similar to The Battle of Polytopia. Client-side, local persistence.

## Architecture
- Pure client-side game. Engine in `/app/frontend/src/game/` (types, data, rng, grid, combat, mapgen, factory, engine, ai, store). UI in `/app/frontend/src/components/`. Screens in `/app/frontend/app/`.
- 2.5D isometric SVG map (diamond tiles, pyramid mountains, 3D unit pawns/city houses/bulls), 4-angle rotation, pan/zoom.

## Implemented (latest)
- **Difficulty**: Setup selector — Peaceful / Easy / Normal / Hard. Peaceful bots prioritise trading and only fight defensively (attack when a player unit is adjacent/in range); Easy is timid; Normal balanced; Hard aggressive with bonus star income. Difficulty is stored on game state and drives `ai.ts`.
- **Trade-line tech pricing**: base costs set to Roads = 2, Construction = 5, Trading = 4, Trading Overseas = 15 (locked tech nodes now show their cost alongside the lock icon).
- **Quick Tutorial**: first-run 5-step overlay (Welcome → Move → Battle/Capture → Cities → Tech) with Next/Back/Skip and progress dots; auto-shows once (flag `hextribes_tutorial_seen_v1`) and re-openable from the pause menu → "How to Play".
- **Merchant UX fix**: units garrisoned on a city are now shown as a badge beside the city; tapping a city then tapping it again selects the garrisoned unit to move it out; Merchants are trained ready-to-move; single-tap a merchant = move options, double-tap a merchant = open its trade/inventory panel.
- **Unit move animation**: units glide tile→tile (~300ms); reachable/attackable tiles highlighted more boldly.
- **City level-up reward modal**: human cities enqueue a reward choice on level-up (Workshop / Treasury / Wall / Grand Park); AI auto-resolves.
- **Shareable victory card**: win/lose result renders a score card (tribe, turns, cities, techs, score) with a native Share button.
- **Naval & Ports (manual embark)**: build a Port on coastal water; land units embark → Rowing Boat → Sailing Boat → Battleship; auto-disembark on land.
- **Roads & Clear-Forest**: build **Roads** (needs Roads tech) on land — units chain along connected roads for free (fast travel); roads render as brown segments linking adjacent road tiles. **Clear Forest** (needs Construction) burns a forest tile into farmable grassland (adds a crop).
- **Merchants & Trading**: train a **Merchant** (needs Trading; **Merchant Ship** = embarked merchant, 8 slots vs 4 on land). Open its **Trade** panel to load goods and set a per-unit price (1–20★). Each round rival (bot) tribes buy your cargo — you earn the price in stars. You can also **buy from another player's merchant**: tap an enemy merchant to open its shop (BuyMerchantPanel) and choose **how many units** to buy per slot via a − / + / MAX stepper (capped by stock and what you can afford); the Buy button shows the live total cost, and purchasing pays stars to its owner. AI tribes with Trading train and stock a merchant. Merchants can't be attacked. **Sale notifications**: at the start of a turn, sales made by that player's own merchant that round are aggregated and shown in a `SaleModal` (only the merchant's owner is notified) — listing quantity sold per good and total stars earned (engine tracks `state.pendingSales` per player; cleared via `doClearSale`).
- **Peaceful capture**: tapping an *enemy city* opens a `CaptureModal` to buy it for **25 stars × its level** (discovered cities); a city can be bought **even if a military unit is garrisoned in it** — the purchase is peaceful, the occupying unit is **left untouched (not attacked or removed)**, and any of the previous owner's units standing **within that city's territory (its tile + neighbours) defect to the buyer** (`defectCityUnits`). When you buy a garrisoned city, a toast announces how many units joined your side. Tapping a *neutral village* offers an instant buy for **15 stars**. Buying transfers/founds the city immediately **for bot-owned cities** (bots implicitly accept and are paid the stars). **When you buy a city from another human player (pass-and-play)**, it instead sends an **offer**: your stars are escrowed and, on the seller's turn, they get an `OfferModal` showing how many stars they'd earn with **Accept** / **Decline** — accept transfers the city and pays the seller, decline refunds you (engine: `requestBuyCity` / `resolveCityOffer`, `state.pendingOffers`). The buyer sees a brief **"Offer sent"** toast confirming the deal is awaiting the seller's reply. Military capture (walking a unit in / defeating the defender) still works and is instant. (AI annexation only targets *undefended* cities.)
- **Village capture takes time**: moving a unit onto a neutral village now starts a *claim* (village tints to the claimer's colour) that only becomes a city at the start of the claimer's next turn, and only if a friendly unit still occupies it. Buying the village (15★) is the sole instant option.
- **Buildings & production**: tap a cell in your territory to build production structures that yield goods/stars each turn — Lumber Hut, Wheat/Bull/Horse Farm, Coal/Iron/Gold Mine. **Mountain ores**: each mountain rolls an ore on map-gen — 40% coal, 30% iron, 10% gold, 20% barren (no mine possible). A mine can only be built on a mountain carrying its matching ore (Coal Mine→coal / +2 pop, Iron Mine→iron ore / +2 iron/turn, Gold Mine→gold / +5 stars/turn); building the mine consumes the ore. Production credited at turn start; AI also builds. Buildings show as markers on the map.
- **Goods economy**: players hold wood/iron/wheat/meat/horses (second top-bar row). Unit training requires goods per the table; city chips show goods costs (red when short).
- Tribes: Lesnoi, Freemen, He-he, Fishmen; biome-specific procedural world-gen (Voronoi regions); map types Continents/Pangea/Lakes/Dryland/Archipelago.
- **New Game flow (screens)**: Main Menu → **Game Mode** (`app/mode.tsx`: centered *Single Player* above *Multiplayer Offline*). Single Player → **Create Game** (`setup.tsx`, vs AI) → Game. Multiplayer Offline → **Create Map** (`create-map.tsx`: map size, map kind, players 2–4, then *Next*) → **Choose Tribe** (`choose-tribe.tsx`, shown once per player in order; each picks a tribe then *Next*, last player taps *Start Game*) → pass-&-play Game. The number of tribes/regions on the map equals the number of players chosen; per-player tribes are passed to `generateGame` via `config.tribes` (duplicates allowed).
- **Game Kind (multiplayer)**: the Create Map screen offers **Open Game** vs **Closed Game**. In a **Closed Game** (`state.closed`): (1) a full-screen **Start-turn gate** shows the upcoming player's label + a centered **Start** button before each turn (so the previous player can't peek), and (2) **fog of war is per-player** — each player only sees the territory they've explored. Per-player exploration is tracked in `tile.seenBy`; `revealFor`/`applyFogForPlayer`/`refreshFog` in engine keep the visible `explored` flags in sync with the current player. Map sizes: Small 12×12, Normal 24×24, Large 36×36. Capitals always spawn with ≥5 land cells around them; neutral villages never spawn on mountains. **Mountain density is capped per tribe area** — the map is scanned in non-overlapping 3×3 chunks and surplus mountains are demoted to that chunk's dominant-tribe terrain: Lesnoi ≤1, Freemen ≤2, He-he ≤3, Fishmen ≤1 mountain per chunk (`MOUNTAIN_CAP` in mapgen).
- Branching radial tech tree (~29 techs). Units: warrior/archer/beefeater/catapult/rider/armored_rider/knight/pikeman/swordsman/merchant + naval tiers.
- **Seafaring AI**: coastal bots now prioritise the naval tech line (fishing→sailing), build a **port** bordering their land, **embark** units sitting on a port when their nearest objective is across water, and sail toward it (auto-disembark on landing) — so oceans and trade routes stay active.
- **AI annexation**: a wealthy bot will peacefully **buy a weak, undefended, nearby rival city** (level ≤ 2, keeping a stars buffer) each turn, adding late-game pressure.
- **Peaceful (economic) victory**: if the human comes to own **every city on the map** while at least one rival still has units (i.e. taken over by buying, not destroying), the game ends as a **"Capital Win"** — the human wins peacefully and no one is defeated (`state.peacefulWin`; VictoryCard shows the shared-win framing).
- **Claim progress ring**: a village being claimed shows a small hourglass ring in the claimer's colour ("captures next turn").
- **HUD**: the top pill shows your **star income per turn** (e.g. "+3"), while your **actual star balance** now sits in the resources panel alongside wood/iron/wheat/meat/horses (`starIncome()` in engine). Every time a city **reaches a new level it permanently produces +1 star per turn** (base bonus applied in `addPopulation`, independent of the chosen level-up reward). City panel opens even when a unit occupies it.
- **Combat defense bonuses** (multiply the defender's DEF, in `combat.ts` `defenseBonus`): open ground ×1; **forest ×1** normally, **×1.5 once Forest Care is researched**; **mountain ×1.25**. Inside your **own city**: basic units ×1 (×2 with a Wall); the **Knight** (defensive unit) ×2 (×4 with a Wall).
- **Roads**: a land unit **standing on a road tile moves twice as far** that turn (move ×2, in `grid.ts` `reachableTiles`); roads still carry units through forest/mountain without stopping. When a unit standing on a road is selected, the **extra tiles it can reach thanks to the road bonus are highlighted brighter** (lighter gold fill + white outline) to distinguish them from its normal move range (`roadExtra` in game.tsx / GameMap).
- **Draw / stalemate**: if the human still holds a city but has **no military units** and **every civilian (merchant) unit is boxed in** (all surrounding cells blocked by enemy units / impassable terrain), a 3-turn countdown starts. A gold HUD banner warns "Draw in N turns". If nothing changes for 3 of the human's turns the game ends in a **DRAW** (`state.status === "draw"`, shown on the VictoryCard). Any change — a freed cell, a newly trained military unit, or a civilian that can move again — resets the countdown and play continues. Logic: `isHumanStalemated` / `stalemateTurnsLeft` in `engine.ts`, evaluated at the start of each human turn in `startPlayerTurn`.
- **Fishing (fish tiles)**: tap a **fish** resource tile inside your own city borders (requires the **Fishing tech** and the tile must be discovered) to open a `FishChoiceModal` — **Hire a Fisherman** (2★, instant +1 pop to nearest city) or **Fish Yourself** (free 3D first-person fishing mini-game). Mini-game (`FishingMiniGame.tsx`, react-three-fiber + expo-gl, locked landscape): 3 worms; CAST the line, wait for the bobber to dip, then PULL within a 1.5s reaction window to catch (`fishSuccess` → +1 pop to nearest city). Missing all worms = no reward. Fishable tiles show the same soft pulsing golden ring as huntable bulls (`canFish` gate in engine; wired in `app/game.tsx` `doHireFisherman`/`doFishSuccess`). WebGL renders in preview but is best validated on a real device / Expo Go.
- **Hunting wild animals**: tap a wild animal (Bull) tile in your territory to open a choice modal — **Hire a Hunter** (3★, instant +1 pop & +1 meat to nearest city) or **Hunt Yourself** (free 3D first-person archery mini-game). Mini-game (`HuntingMiniGame.tsx`, react-three-fiber + expo-gl, locked landscape): player 12 HP / 12 arrows; bull charges & gores for 4 HP (with strafing dodges); hitboxes — head = 1-shot kill, body = 4 hits, legs = 12 hits. Killing the bull grants +1 pop & +1 meat to the nearest city; dying or running out of arrows = no reward. Hunting requires the **Hunting tech** and the animal must lie **inside your own city borders** (tapping bulls outside your territory or without the tech does nothing — enforced by `canHunt`). Huntable bulls display a soft **pulsing golden ring** on the map so players can see which ones are tappable. Wired in `app/game.tsx` (`doHireHunter` / `doHuntSuccess`). Note: WebGL renders in preview but is best validated on a real device / Expo Go.

- **Sound effects & settings toggles**: bundled offline SFX (`assets/sounds/`, generated by `scripts/gen_sfx.py` — no downloads) played via **expo-audio**: `tap` (UI selections), `battle` (attacks), `trade` (buying from a merchant + own-merchant sale notification), `coin` (city/village purchase via CaptureModal + city sale when an OfferModal is accepted). Manager in `src/utils/fx.ts` (`initFx` primes players at app start in `_layout.tsx`, `playSfx`, `haptic` wrapper). Three persisted settings in the **pause menu** — **Sound Effects** toggle, an **SFX volume slider** (`@react-native-community/slider`, 0–100%, master gain applied on top of each sfx's base volume, `hextribes_sfx_vol_v1`), and a **Vibration** toggle — stored in AsyncStorage (`hextribes_sound_on_v1` / `hextribes_haptics_on_v1`); the `haptic` wrapper gates every vibration call (game.tsx + both mini-games) so turning Vibration off silences all haptics. Reactive via `useFxSettings` (useSyncExternalStore).

- **3D unit models**: Warrior (`warrior`), Archer (`archer`), Swordsman (`swordsmen`), Rider (`rider`), Knight (`chivalry`), Pikeman (`pikemen`), Merchant (`merchant`), Catapult (`catapult`), Armored Rider (`armored_rider`) and Beefeater (`beefeater`) render on the map as pre-rendered low-poly **3D sprites** (from the user's `rider.obj` + FBX models) instead of the flat SVG pawn, each with its unit icon floating above. Sprites are tinted per tribe (`assets/images/<type>/…`). FBX models carry per-vertex colours (converted to glTF via `assimp`): already-coloured parts are kept, and the artist's **blank region — the default grey ~0.8 — is recoloured to the tribe colour** (`scripts/render_models.py`; `render_knight.py` / `render_rider.py` are the originals). All rendering is flat-shaded offline with matplotlib, so there's no runtime 3D cost. **All models are rendered at one shared world scale with a common feet baseline** (`scripts/render_all.py`); mounted units are additionally shrunk in 2D and re-grounded via the `SCALE` map (rider 0.5, armored_rider 0.5, chivalry/Knight 0.25) so they read at foot-unit size. Wired generically in `GameMap.tsx` via `MODEL_SPRITES` (keyed by unit type) + `modelSprite()`; the SVG pawn is skipped for any type in `MODEL_SPRITES`, and an `<Image>` is drawn in both the static token overlay and the glide/anim token. Embarked units (with `unit.boat`) still show the boat, not the model.

- **Peaceful difficulty is truly peaceful**: peaceful bots never strike first. `Player.provoked` (types.ts) is set on a defender's owner inside `engine.attackUnit` whenever a unit is attacked by a different owner; `ai.ts` only fights when `cfg.aggressive || provoked` (peaceful has `aggressive:false`), so a peaceful bot stays passive until the human attacks one of its units, then retaliates. Covered by `scripts/engine_test.js` (77/77): "peaceful bot does NOT strike first", "attacking a peaceful bot provokes it", "provoked peaceful bot fights back". Verified by testing_agent (iteration_7).

- **Expo SDK upgraded 54 → 57** (expo 57.0.19, react-native 0.86.3; `expo-doctor` 20/20). Breaking changes handled: removed `newArchEnabled`/`edgeToEdgeEnabled` from `app.json`; migrated icons from `@expo/vector-icons` (`import { MaterialCommunityIcons }`) to `@react-native-vector-icons/material-design-icons` (default import, same local name, identical MDI glyph names) across all 26 screens/components; added its config plugin (auto). The old Expo-Go CDN font hack (`use-icon-fonts.ts` + splash gate in `_layout.tsx`) was removed — `@react-native-vector-icons/common` now auto-loads the bundled `MaterialDesignIcons.ttf` at runtime via ExpoFontLoader (works in Expo Go). Added missing peer dep `expo-asset`. Verified: icons render in menu + game, engine tests 77/77.
- **Post-upgrade fix — 3D mini-games (Hunting/Fishing) blank WebGL Canvas**: after the SDK 57 upgrade the `@react-three/fiber` mini-games rendered nothing (map showed through). Cause: Metro package-exports (default since SDK 55) resolved `three` via two entry files → "Multiple instances of Three.js" → the WebGLRenderer produced no output. Fix in `metro.config.js`: a `resolver.resolveRequest` shim forces every bare `three` import to a single file (`node_modules/three/build/three.cjs`). Verified by testing_agent (iteration_8): both mini-games render their 3D scenes; general post-upgrade smoke passes. (Note: End Turn testID is `action-end-turn`.)
